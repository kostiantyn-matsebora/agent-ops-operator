  - A LATER dispatch, on `73f61216` (run 38042832736, after 7.3 landed), FAILED both lanes — and found something real: `TestReplicasTwoDeliversEveryConsoleThread`'s OWN precondition (`len(pods) >= 2`, no readiness check) had been passing for as long as this lane existed against only ONE ready manager pod, on this same single-node k3d cluster.   - A THIRD dispatch, on `d2b92343` (run 38044690645), FAILED again — and cascaded into several unrelated lanes failing too (`TestContextSurvivesLosingThePod`, `TestAdmissionFIFOOnPodDelete`, `TestStubMechanisms/*`). See 7.3 for both root causes and the fix.
- [x] 7.3 Harsher sibling added after a live-cluster run (5.2) on a local 3-replica install found 2.6–2.8: `TestReplicasThreeDeliversEveryConsoleThreadInParallel`.
  - THREE replicas, not the chart's default two.
  - TRUE concurrency — goroutines, not a sequential loop, so two polls can race at the same instant.
  - A stronger assertion than 7.1's: a real thread AND a delivered reply, not merely a thread. 7.1 could not have caught 2.8's race.
  - `assertManagerRunsAtLeastNReadyReplicas` generalizes 7.1's precondition to check READY pods, not merely listed ones — the check that caught the gap 7.2 records.
  - `scaleManagerReplicas` forces the manager Deployment to N real, ready replicas on this pack's single-node k3d cluster, which the chart's own hard pod anti-affinity otherwise makes impossible past one. 7.1 is updated to call it too, for the same reason.
  - **Patching the Deployment alone was tried first and is not enough.** A pod's spec is immutable once created, so an EXISTING pod still carries the old anti-affinity rule after the Deployment's template is patched, and the scheduler still refuses to place a new pod beside it — measured live as the rollout stalling at "1 out of N new replicas updated," on both a local Rancher Desktop cluster and this exact e2e pack in CI (7.2's failed run). Deleting the blocking pods was tried next and also failed: their OWNING ReplicaSet's desired count is untouched by that, so it recreates them, identical, within seconds. The fix scales that ReplicaSet itself to zero — identified by `spec.template.spec.affinity != nil`, never the current one — which is the only thing that stops it recreating them.
  - **Scaling the old ReplicaSet to zero was still not fast enough.** That only asks its pod to terminate GRACEFULLY — up to 30s — and the pod still carries the old anti-affinity rule, still blocking the new ones, until it is actually gone. Measured live on the 7.2 dispatch that found this: the rollout still timed out, now reporting "0 of N updated replicas are available" rather than "1 out of N updated" — scheduling was no longer stuck, but the old pod's termination window ate the whole 3-minute budget. Fixed by force-deleting (`client.GracePeriodSeconds(0)`) the old ReplicaSet's pods right after zeroing it, rather than waiting out their shutdown — a test fixture has no reason to wait for a pod on its way out regardless. Verified by hand: 23s end to end against the reproduced stall, versus the multi-minute hang before.
  - **A cleanup-ordering bug made the SAME failed dispatch worse: it cascaded.** `scaleManagerReplicas` registered its restore `t.Cleanup` AFTER the risky scale-up call. `t.Fatal` stops the calling goroutine at once via `runtime.Goexit`, so a scale-up that failed skipped the registration entirely — the Deployment was left stuck mid-rollout for every test that ran afterward, and several entirely unrelated lanes failed as a result. Fixed by registering the cleanup FIRST, unconditionally, before the call it is meant to clean up after.
  - **The scheduler's OWN retry-after-failure did not reliably fire, even after the blocking pod was gone.** Deleting the old ReplicaSet's pods happens AFTER the Deployment's patch already asked for the new ones, so a new pod's FIRST scheduling attempt can still lose that race and fail with the same anti-affinity error — then sit in the scheduler's backoff queue. Measured live: a `FailedScheduling` event, and no second attempt for 4+ minutes after the blocking pod was long gone. Fixed by force-deleting any still-PENDING pod of the Deployment's CURRENT ReplicaSet right after clearing the old one, forcing a FRESH pod object with an ordinary, un-backed-off scheduling attempt — never a Running pod, and never when no old-affinity ReplicaSet was actually cleared (see next finding for why that scoping matters).
  - **The first version of that fix was too broad, and killed a healthy pod.** It deleted ANY Pending pod of the current ReplicaSet, including one from an ORDINARY scale-up with no competing affinity rule at all — nothing for it to lose a race against. Measured live: a brand-new, correctly-scheduling replica force-deleted before the scheduler had even placed it once, reporting `Completed` after the manager's own graceful-shutdown path ran. Fixed by gating the delete on whether an old-affinity ReplicaSet was actually cleared in THIS call.
  - **A second, unrelated bug surfaced once the first was fixed: `TestReplicasThreeDeliversEveryConsoleThreadInParallel` intermittently left one of its 10 conversations waiting minutes for an admission slot.** The preceding lane's 8 conversations stay Idle after it returns, each still holding a slot under `maxActiveConversations` (5 by default) until their own idle TTL evicts them — so a lane immediately after it can face up to 18 conversations competing for 5 pod slots. Measured live: `"evicting idle worker to make room"` logged for a conversation that had done nothing wrong but arrive behind a still-occupied backlog. Bumping the test's own wait timeout was tried first and is the wrong fix — the delay is not bounded, it grows with how much of the preceding lane's backlog is still live. Fixed by having each lane close its own conversations and WAIT for the close to actually land (phase `Closed`) before returning — a fire-and-forget `/close` is not enough, since the next lane's conversations can be created before the reconciler catches up.
  - **CI dispatch found the fix above was still not enough: the backlog was never only this file's.** The admission cap is GLOBAL across every source, not scoped to console, and a sibling lane on `vm-alerts`, `tg-ops` or `e2e-fanout` that never calls `/close` leaves its own conversation Idle too. Measured on CI: 7 such leftovers from unrelated lanes, still competing for 5 slots. Worse, `e2e-cron` keeps firing on its own schedule independent of any test's lifetime, so a one-time sweep at the top of a lane can still lose the race to a NEW cron-originated conversation created mid-run — measured as the same starvation recurring intermittently even with a sweep in place. Fixed by reusing `TestAdmissionFIFOOnPodDelete`'s own `pauseCronLaneAndClearLeftovers` (un-claims the cron source so its ticks stop admitting anything, then deletes every existing conversation) in BOTH replica lanes, each restoring the pipeline on its own cleanup.
  - **That reuse needed its own fix: the existing helper's delete runs with `--wait=false`.** A deleted conversation lingers under its close-topics finalizer for up to two minutes, still holding a slot the whole time. Calling the helper and immediately creating a new batch, as this file first tried, raced the OLD conversations' finalizers against the NEW batch's own close-and-wait step and timed out. `TestAdmissionFIFOOnPodDelete` itself follows the pause with its own explicit wait for zero `agentops.dev/conversation` pods, and `clearAdmissionPoolForReplicaLane` adds the same wait here.
  - **A THIRD, entirely separate bug was exposed — not introduced — by getting past the first two: the test harness's long-lived port-forward to the manager Service does not survive the pod churn this file's own replica-scaling causes.** `kubectl port-forward svc/agentops-manager` pins itself to one backing pod's IP for its whole life. `scaleManagerReplicas`'s force-deletes make that pod disappear out from under it, and the forwarding process exits with nothing to restart it. Every later test sharing that same `Forward` then fails `connection refused` against a port nothing listens on — measured live cascading into `TestContextSurvivesLosingThePod`, `TestAdmissionFIFOOnPodDelete` and `TestStubMechanisms/*`, all unrelated to replicas. Master's own smoke suite never does this pod churn, so it was never exposed there — this change's own new test code is what surfaces it. Fixed in `test/e2e/cluster.go`'s `Forward`: a supervisor goroutine relaunches the port-forward on the same local port whenever its process exits before `Stop` is called.
  - **A FOURTH bug, in `zeroAffinityReplicaSets` itself, surfaced only when a replica lane runs standalone (no earlier lane having already cleared the affinity field first).** The function listed ReplicaSets exactly once, immediately after patching the Deployment — but the Deployment controller does not necessarily have the NEW ReplicaSet (the one with nil affinity) created in that same instant. Finding none yet left `current` nil, silently skipping the fresh-reschedule step for every pod the new ReplicaSet went on to create. Measured live: three pods stuck on their one and only `FailedScheduling` event for 5+ minutes, with the OLD ReplicaSet already cleanly zeroed — proof the rest of the function had done its job and only the timing of this one list call was wrong. Fixed by retrying the list, up to 30s, until the new ReplicaSet actually exists.
  - **A FIFTH finding, this one CI-only and bounded, not a regression from the four fixes above.** Dispatching the fourth fix's own head still saw 2 of 10 conversations sit with NO reconciler activity naming them at all — not even a log line — until a claim finally landed at ~178s, almost exactly two `claimStalenessSeconds` cycles (90s default). The claim's own holder pod had a nearly empty log for its whole life, consistent with leader-election churn from `scaleManagerReplicas`'s own force-deletes landing right as this lane starts, which CI's slower, shared runner exposes and a fast local cluster does not. UNLIKE the admission-starvation bug's symptom, this one is BOUNDED (two cycles, not growing across reruns), so widening this lane's own wait to 5m is the right fix here, not the wrong one it was for that bug.
  - Verified live against the local Rancher Desktop cluster (10/10 conversations, thread + delivery, in 10-14s across repeated runs) before being written back into this binary, against TWO consecutive full local runs of this package's entire smoke suite (not just these two tests in isolation — the admission-starvation and port-forward bugs only reproduce with every sibling lane running) exiting clean with zero failures, and against `TestReplicasThreeDeliversEveryConsoleThreadInParallel` run standalone — the exact reproduction of the fourth bug. The fifth finding is CI-environment-specific and does not reproduce locally, so its own confirmation is the NEXT `e2e-smoke.yml` dispatch on this change's final head — this task's own completion evidence.
